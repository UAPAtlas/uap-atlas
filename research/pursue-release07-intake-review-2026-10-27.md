## High-value primary findings

1. **2026 triangle: one event family, two documentary channels.** [AARO D176](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release-07/documents/DOW-UAP-D176-Triangular_UAP_AARO_MFR_2026.pdf) pp. 1–3 and [FBI D043](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release-07/documents/FBI-UAP-D043-FD_302_Unidentified_Aerial_Object_Observation_Report-2026.pdf) pp. 1–2 share the distinctive witness/radio/terrain sequence, but are **not two independent confirmations**. D176 p. 3 expressly says there was **no instrumented speed measurement**. D043 p. 1 describes a shape “most resembling a delta wing drone.” Crucially, D043 p. 1 reads approximately **1923 hours** and p. 2 calls the *total* sighting approximately **three seconds**; D176 pp. 1–3 reads **2023L**, approximately **10 seconds ascent, 20 seconds hover, 2–3 seconds terminal flight**. These differences must remain unresolved, not silently harmonized. D043 pp. 3–11 contain illustrative-looking landscape/triangle attachments without demonstrated native-capture metadata. Neither released file supplies native thermal video or calibrated speed/acceleration. This is a distinct new-case candidate, not an existing triangle-case match.
2. **BAASS Utah Ranch: actual mundane sample result.** [D174](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release-07/documents/DOW-UAP-D174-Utah_Ranch_Investigation_Report_April_2010.pdf) p. 37 reports three submitted hair samples sharing a canine HVI sequence common in domestic dogs; BAASS p. 38 interprets the fourth as raccoon. That result is specific to those samples, not a general resolution of reported ranch events. The report also records witness claims and plant experiments, but no demonstrated anomalous organism in these tested samples. Strong program-history enrichment candidate for `BF-SF-09`.
3. **Nimitz modeled performance is not a fresh measurement.** [BAASS D175](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release-07/documents/DOW-UAP-D175-BAASS_Self-Assessment_of_UAP_Analysis_Capabilities_November_2010.pdf) p. 33 explicitly acknowledges little hard data, no exact duration, and testimony plus a 0.5–10-second assumed range. Extreme speed/acceleration calculations on pp. 34–35 are conditional; CFD on pp. 40–48 is simulation, not event imagery or recovered SPY-1 tracking. Material limitation for `BF-2004-NM-01` and program dossier `BF-SF-09`.
4. **BAASS institutional chain is real, premises remain claims.** D168 pp. 1–2 gives contract `HHM402-08-C-0072`; pp. 10–11 requests access to allegedly held films/materials rather than proving transfer. D169 p. 22's disclosure text belongs to hypothetical scenarios. D171 p. 115 flags weak physical-item custody in the Brazil work, and pp. 119–131 Northern Tier witness/recollection limitations. D167 pp. 5–7 cautions against overlapping catalogues and built-in extraterrestrial assumptions. These records strengthen program provenance, not the truth of every contractor assertion.
5. **Historical event candidates require differentiated treatment.** D155 p. 1 has a 1957 Saudi B-52 gunnery-scope return, with disappearance during an aircraft bank; pp. 2/4 are drawings rather than radar film. D156 pp. 1–4 has a Guam F-86D visual report alongside a documented balloon launch and balloon alternative. D158 pp. 1/3 has two Prince George airport guards' report without radar/optical corroboration. These were separately adjudicated and admitted as bounded historical dossiers `BF-1957-SA-01`, `BF-1956-GU-01`, and `BF-1953-PG-01`. D163 is press-mediated Swedish DC-3 testimony, not a confirmed radar track.
6. **Some “unresolved” filenames conceal conventional assessments.** Catalina D177 pp. 1/4 favors meteor; D178 p. 1 explicitly concludes Jupiter. D179 p. 2 references scout letters/sketches not held in its two-page file. D181 pp. 1/3 pairs a six-light card with a letter reporting a later four-light episode. These are separate 1949/1952/1953/1955 event families, not one Catalina case.
7. **Space-fragment policy is not proof of anomalous retrieval.** [EOP D002](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release-07/documents/EOP-UAP-D002-Memo_on_Interagency_Coordination_for_the_Recovery_of_Foreign_Materiel-May_1963.pdf) p. 1 documents Hale's account of Frutkin's “cover” concern. The companion [USG D002](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release-07/documents/USG-UAP-D002-Interagency_Correspondence_Concerning_Space_Fragments-1962_1964.pdf) pp. 18–31 documents a genuine NASA/DoD/CIA dispute over foreign-launched space-vehicle fragments, including draft DoD access to “whole vehicles.” The presidential approval formula on pp. 53–55 is in an **unnumbered draft NSAM**, not proof of implementation. Pp. 23–24 show disagreement persisted. Withdrawal sheets pp. 1–2 identify November 1963 draft material not present in the released packet; no final signed policy, unusual specimen, UAP recovery or chain of custody is established. EOP and USG are one policy-correspondence family, not independent alien-retrieval confirmations.
8. **D173 corrects the most tempting readings of BAASS operations.** [November 2009 report](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release-07/documents/DOW-UAP-D173-BAASS_Monthly_Report_November_2009.pdf) pp. 5–10 resolves the Modesto blue-light case as model gliders, while rash causation remains unknown. Port Jervis pp. 40–50 and the Central Texas November 2009 cluster pp. 19–39 are distinct *testimony/field-method candidates*, not the January 2008 Stephenville case or measured anomalous vehicles. Ranch seed-germination pp. 107–113 ends in a blank results sheet; p. 114 itself lists officer fraud, trespassers and animals as alternatives for coin anecdotes. P. 126 retracts the preceding researcher-threat assessment; p. 155 says classified-archive/Northern Tier work was on hold for lack of Need to Know. The medical summary in the blue-orb section p. 143 is redacted; released pages do not independently establish an orb-caused injury.
9. **D180 is a personnel record, not the missing Estimate.** [McCoy file](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release-07/documents/DOW-UAP-D180_Final-Personnel-Record-for-McCoy-Howard-M-US-Air-Force-1954.pdf) pp. 258–266 confirms senior AMC technical-intelligence duties under Twining across 1947–49. The p. 92 succession order names Watson as his replacement but gives no UFO-related cause; pp. 69–70 identify classified Science Survey Office functions only generically, with p. 100–102 examples of sound equipment and investigative photography. Full-file text screening found no explicit Project SIGN/GRUDGE reference, Estimate of the Situation, or specific UAP incident. This strengthens biography/organization, not an alleged extraterrestrial conclusion or suppression narrative.
