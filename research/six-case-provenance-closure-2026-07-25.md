## BF-1986-JAL-01 — Japan Airlines 1628

**Primary custody:** FAA Alaskan Region case packet; NARA Catalog record 40587205; Atlas-local chronology, certified ATC transcript, and releases 87-08/87-09.

**Verified quotation:** “The ROCC called ZAN advising they had confirmed a ‘flight of two’ in JL1628's position.”

**Locator:** FAA Anchorage ARTCC chronology, Dec. 18, 1986, packet p. 4.

**Supports:** Real-time crew/ATC handling; intermittent ROCC primary-return reports; FAA inquiry and split-return analysis.

**Boundary:** The visual report, aircraft weather radar, and intermittent ground returns do not form a continuous common track. A diverted United crew saw JAL but no other traffic. FAA’s split-return explanation addresses one radar indication but does not identify the visual stimulus.

## BF-2004-NM-01 — Nimitz / Tic Tac

**Mapped records:** Page-level public copy of the 13-page executive summary; official DoD/DVIDS FLIR1 media record, video 955825.

**Verified quotation:** “The AAVs would descend ‘very rapidly’ from approximately 60,000 feet down to approximately 50 feet in a matter of seconds.”

**Locator:** Executive-summary public copy, p. 1.

**Supports:** Reported USS Princeton detections; F/A-18 visual intercept and object description; official DoD custody of the released FLIR1 media.

## BF-1980-PE-01 — La Joya Intercept

**Primary custody:** Declassified Department of Defense/JCS Message Center copy; USDAO Lima information report `IR 6 876 0146 80`; Atlas-local page and 12-page DIA collection PDF.

**Verified quotation:** “The FAP tried to intercept and destroy the UFO, but without success.”

**Locator:** Message p. 1 / collection PDF p. 11; DTG 022052Z JUN 80; received June 3, 1980.

**Supports:** Official-message documentation of two reported sightings near a southern Peru air base; reported intercept attempt; source described as an observing Peruvian Air Force officer positioned to know related conversations and reliable in prior reporting.

**Boundary:** The page explicitly states that it is “not finally evaluated intelligence.” The native Peruvian operational file, weapons record, gun-camera film, and pilot debrief are not public. Later detailed engagement claims remain testimony-derived.

**Primary custody:** CIA Information Report `OO-B-93674`, “Report of Unusual Flying Object Sightings and Attendant Scientific Activity”; the complete three-page PDF and all released page images are local. PDF SHA-256: `1f6d1a95647dbd14d34534db4c71654e4b437c61a160df24fc142ef60790b730`.
